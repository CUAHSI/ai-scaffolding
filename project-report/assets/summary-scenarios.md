# Project-report summary acceptance scenarios (CAM-1624, CAM-1625)

Exercise these at the **skill invocation** seam: give the reporting agent a root or label selector, dates, and synthetic Jira records below (or equivalent read-only test records), then inspect the report in chat and the local Markdown artifact. The first section targets the default `summary` format; the CAM-1625 section also covers delivery in other formats. These fixtures are not evidence for a real report. The CLI collector's pagination and date conversion have executable tests in `tests/test_project_report_collection.py`.

Use a reporting period of 2026-10-01 through 2026-10-31 in `America/New_York` unless specified otherwise. The cutoff is 2026-11-01 04:00Z. Each cited comment below is created before cutoff and unedited unless stated otherwise. The reporting root is an Epic; every listed ticket is its child except where a Sub-task is named.

1. **Boundary and scope.** For March 7–8, 2026 in `America/New_York`, accept an event at `2026-03-07T05:00Z` and one at `2026-03-09T03:59Z`; exclude `2026-03-07T04:59Z` and `2026-03-09T04:00Z`. An end date of today is provisional through its recorded UTC retrieval time. Missing or ambiguous root/dates require clarification; reversed/future-ended dates must not generate a report.
2. **Paged nested work.** The root's search results require two pages; one child has a Sub-task requiring its own child query. A child's comment and changelog each require two pages. A `Relates` link to a different project is not a descendant. All accessible pages and the Sub-task contribute evidence; the linked work does not. No single first page establishes completeness.
3. **Changed and reopened status.** Story A is now `Done`, but its changelog shows `In Progress → Done` on November 2; at cutoff it is **active**, not completed. Story B went `In Progress → Done` on October 12 and `Done → In Progress` on October 20. At cutoff B is **active**, with its reopening explained once, rather than counted as two completions. A lost history page makes the affected historical state unknown, not today's state.
4. **Partial result and event date.** Story C remains `In Progress`; its October 17 comment says “the dataset was delivered October 14.” Include the delivered dataset as a recorded accomplishment dated October 14, with a nearby comment citation and the unresolved Story state. An October 20 comment saying a different result was delivered in September does **not** make it an October accomplishment. A comment written after cutoff cannot establish a cutoff-valid outcome, even if it mentions October.
5. **Outstanding, abandoned, and already finished.** Story D has been `To Do` since August with no October activity: include it as outstanding without calling it a new contribution. Story E transitioned to `Won't Do` on October 23: describe abandonment separately from successful delivery. Story F was `Done` in September with no distinct October outcome: do not present its closure as an October accomplishment.
6. **Parent/child overlap.** Story G and its Sub-task H both transitioned to `Done` in October for the same delivered dataset. Report the result once, backed by the most direct evidence; do not sum two completions or allow G's state to replace H's outcome evidence.
7. **Delivery and safety.** For each output, verify root link, inclusive dates, timezone, retrieval and cutoff timestamps, format, coverage limitation, and nearby Jira citation for every accomplishment. The file and chat contain the same complete report text. Two runs with identical arguments make distinct files, preserving the earlier one. Jira access is GET-only; neither the report nor working evidence is published or committed.

## CAM-1625 partial and empty coverage

Exercise the following at the same request-to-report seam, for each format where relevant. These are synthetic acceptance cases, not Jira findings:

1. **Partial pages and fields.** Root child search page 1 yields Story C, Story D, and Story E (whose issue GET fails); page 2 fails. C's first comment page contains the cutoff-valid October 14 delivery claim from scenario 4, while a later comment page and a changelog page fail (`comments_complete: false`, `changelog_complete: false`). D's requested due date is missing. The collector retains accessible pages and records each failure with `key`, `endpoint`, `page`, `reason` (and `field` for D's missing field). Deliver the supported C outcome with its comment citation, but leave C's affected cutoff lifecycle/assignment and D's historical due date or planning assessment unknown. Mark the report **partial**, naming inaccessible E, the affected comment/history/search pages, and D's missing field; do not infer missing activity or use today's field values.
2. **Cutoff and contradiction.** A post-cutoff comment claiming October delivery and a pre-cutoff comment or description edited after cutoff without an earlier version provide no historical outcome evidence. Confirm unusable comment bodies are absent from the collector's `comments`, with their IDs and reasons retained in `excluded_comments`; assess descriptions against their changelog. If a valid pre-cutoff comment claims C's delivered dataset while C's cutoff workflow remains unresolved, cite the claim and preserve the unresolved or unknown state; neither claim overrides the other. Keep unavailable wording and historical fields explicit in coverage notes.
3. **Empty versus insufficient.** Successful child searches yielding no descendants produce a full qualified report saying no qualifying evidence was found, with the current-membership caveat. Accessible descendants with no qualifying findings and partial comment retrieval produce a **partial empty** report with failed scope named; for a people filter say no matching evidence was found, not that the person did no work. An inaccessible root, a retrieval failure leaving no accessible descendants, or otherwise insufficient evidence stops with an actionable explanation instead of generating an empty report.
4. **Dual delivery and citations.** Ordinary, partial, and empty reports each contain root link, dates, timezone, cutoff/retrieval time, filter, coverage and current-membership qualifications in the complete Markdown and matching chat text. Every supported outcome and attribution has a nearby Jira citation; a partial/empty chat response retains the same citations and caveats rather than summarizing away gaps.
5. **Artifact failures and collisions.** Two runs with identical filename components preserve the first local artifact and create the second exclusively with a numeric suffix. If the destination cannot be written, show the available complete report text in chat, identify the write failure and incomplete delivery, and request a writable destination; do not claim a saved file. A partial Jira report and a failed artifact write are distinct conditions that can coexist.

## CAM-1622 label-selection scenarios

Use the same historical period and synthetic outcomes above, placing descendants under Research Project roots; invoke the skill with a **project label instead of an explicit root**. Keep the existing root scenarios and their filename/collector contract unchanged. For each delivered report, check the label selector, matched project keys/names/links, dates, timezone, cutoff/retrieval time, outcome citations, current-label and descendant-membership caveats, identical full chat/file text, and exclusive `label-<slug>_<start>_<end>_<format>_<UTC>.md` creation. Label evidence bundles contain `project_label`, `matched_projects` (`key`, `summary`), `roots`, flattened `items` with `reporting_root`, `period`, `partial`, `failures`, and `coverage`.

1. **One match.** Current Research Project `CAM-100` (summary `Watershed`) alone carries exact label `WatershedOps`; its Story C has the cited October 14 delivery. Report C under CAM-100 once; an Objective with that label is not a match. Where the invoking repo limits searches to a Jira project, require its key from repo guidance and pass `--project CAM` with `--project-label`. Honor any narrower issue-tree boundary; if repo guidance has no key for a scoped search, ask instead of querying broadly.
2. **Multiple matches and deduplication.** In an unscoped invoking repo, both `CAM-100` (`Watershed`) and `HYDRO-20` (`HydroFabric`) currently carry exact `WatershedOps`. Paginate to find both, include accessible descendants under each, group outcomes by Research Project, and show a shared delivered dataset only once with both project associations and supporting citations. Neither first-match selection nor silently limiting discovery to CAM passes.
3. **No match.** Complete permitted-scope search yields no accessible Research Project with exact `WatershedOps` (even if an Epic carries it). Ask the user to confirm the label spelling/scope; produce neither an empty report nor an artifact. If search pages failed, disclose the gap rather than claim no match.
4. **Ambiguous spelling.** User asks for `watershed ops`, while accessible Research Projects carry distinct labels `WatershedOps` and `watershed-ops`. Ask which exact label they mean before collection or saving; do not silently normalize or combine them. Multiple projects carrying the *same* exact label are not ambiguous spelling.
5. **Partial access.** Exact `WatershedOps` matches CAM-100 and HYDRO-20; CAM-100 issue GET fails, and HYDRO-20 Story C's second comment page and Story D's descendant query fail. Deliver HYDRO-20's supported cited outcome if sufficient, with `partial: true`, failures identifying the inaccessible root and descendant/comment pages, and explicit unknown or unsearched scope; do not assert CAM-100 was inactive or silently drop it. If no root/descendant evidence remains, stop with an actionable explanation instead. State that current label membership (including labels added/removed after cutoff) does not reconstruct historical membership, separately from current-descendant limitations.

A live read-only smoke run should use an accessible Jira hierarchy with an explicit historical period. Compare the output to the source records and disclose current-membership limits even if all reachable pages succeed. Do not copy fixture claims into a live report.
