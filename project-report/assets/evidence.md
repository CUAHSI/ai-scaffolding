# Evidence rules for historical Jira reporting

Apply these rules per item before writing narrative. Keep a working ledger with key, parent, created time, period-end lifecycle (and evidence), qualifying outcomes and outcome dates (and citations), outstanding/abandoned work, commitments (with their recorded times), planning assessment, documented contributors (with citations), assignment at relevant times, and coverage gaps. The ledger is working material, not a second required artifact.

## Historical boundary

- Use Jira timestamps in the reporting timezone to select events on both inclusive calendar dates; compare instants against the cutoff, not date strings or a fixed UTC offset. An end date of today is provisional through the recorded retrieval instant.
- Status, resolution, assignee, and scheduling fields are historical values. When the complete changelog and current field are available, reverse post-cutoff changes (use each change's prior value) to reconstruct cutoff state, then read the earlier history for in-period transitions. Alternatively replay the complete history forward from a reliably established initial state. If a requested field, changelog page, or ordering is missing or inconsistent, mark affected historical values unknown and name the gap; never use today's field as a fallback. Check creation time before attributing any state to the cutoff.
- Current descendant membership is only a discovery method. A parent changed after cutoff can leave historical membership uncertain; a moved-out or deleted issue cannot necessarily be discovered. Disclose the limitation, including for apparently empty reports. Parent completion cannot stand in for undocumented child delivery.
- Use comments and descriptions only as recorded claims. The collector's `comments` retains text with pre-cutoff creation and edit timestamps; its `excluded_comments` retains only IDs/times/reasons, so disclose relevant exclusions without using their bodies. Check descriptions against the complete changelog: exclude text edited after cutoff without a reconstructable earlier version. If creation or edit timing cannot be established, the wording is not cutoff-valid evidence. A description by itself describes intention, not delivery. A dated status change can establish a workflow completion event, but does not independently prove a claimed real-world outcome.
- Separate the date an outcome occurred from the date its supporting evidence was recorded. An in-period statement that explicitly says delivery happened before the period does not create an in-period accomplishment. When the delivery date is not established, do not date it merely by comment creation; describe it as a claim recorded in the period with uncertain delivery date. Preserve material disagreements, e.g. a completion comment alongside an unresolved workflow state.

## Inclusion and lifecycle

- Include outcomes established as achieved within the period, work outstanding at cutoff regardless of activity, and work abandoned during the period. Do not present unchanged outstanding work as a new contribution. Work completed/abandoned before the start is context only unless it contains a distinct supported period outcome.
- For this project's statuses: `To Do`/`Up Next` = not started; `In Progress`/`In Review` = active; `Done` = completed; `Won't Do` = abandoned. Elsewhere use explicit workflow status and resolution meaning; an ambiguous closed state is unknown, not automatically successful. Keep not-started, active, completed, abandoned, and unknown distinct where relevant.
- A ticket completed then reopened in the period appears once under its cutoff state, with the transition noted. Multiple `Done` transitions are not multiple accomplishments. Report independently delivered outcomes within it if supported; an unfinished parent does not block a child outcome, and a completed parent and child do not double-count the same delivered result.
- For a partial accomplishment inside an unresolved ticket, cite the particular delivered outcome and retain the unresolved container state. Group related results by significance rather than reproducing every ticket as an activity log.

## Planning is independent of lifecycle

- Treat work as planned only if a dated schedule or explicit dated commitment recorded by cutoff can be established historically (including field histories or reliable dated text). Sprint membership, rank, `Up Next`, and vague terms such as “soon” do not prove a deadline.
- Use the latest unambiguous cutoff-valid completion commitment; disclose material earlier misses and revisions. If commitments conflict and the latest cannot be determined, flag the conflict rather than picking a date. A current due date entered after cutoff is not a historical plan.
- Completed by cutoff but delivered after an established deadline: lifecycle **completed**, planning **completed late**, when evidence establishes both dates. Still unresolved after a recorded completion deadline: lifecycle **active/not started/unknown** as supported, planning **not completed as planned** (including an older commitment, without calling it a new-period failure). Otherwise say the plan is not established or its assessment is unknown. Cancellation is **abandoned** with its documented reason, not automatically missed delivery; report a missed commitment only when independently supported.

## Attribution and filter

- A **documented contribution** needs an explicit Jira statement identifying who did the work. Self-reports and explicit credit to others both qualify as recorded claims; keep their qualifications, support shared credit, and cite the statement near the person and outcome. A comment's author is not automatically the person who did the described work.
- **Assignment-based attribution** is a weaker category: for completed work use assignment at the established accomplishment date, and for outstanding work use assignment at cutoff. If assignment at that time is unknown, say unknown, not today's assignee. Creator/reporter, mentions, comments, and transition authorship do not confer delivery credit.
- Resolve filters against stable Jira account IDs where possible. Identical display names are not identity evidence; ask the user to disambiguate. Explicitly named non-account contributors may be retained by name with uncertain matches visible. With a filter, include work tied by documented contribution or historical assignment to the selected people and enough collaborator context for honest shared credit; omit unrelated work. If nothing matches, say **no matching evidence**, not “this person contributed nothing.” Without a filter, preserve unknown-contributor work.

## Coverage and citation check

Before delivery, check each finding against its Jira source and temporal validity: each accomplishment has a nearby comment permalink when available (Jira comment `#comment-<id>` or the site's supported comment link), otherwise a ticket URL plus a dated history event; each personal attribution is independently sourced. Use retained pages of incomplete comment or history collections only for claims they directly support; missing comments cannot establish no outcomes or credit, and missing relevant history makes affected states, plans, or assignment unknown. In every format, including an empty report, name inaccessible tickets, endpoints/pages, missing requested fields, unavailable historical text, identity ambiguities, contradictions, and unsupported claims as applicable. Mark retrieval gaps **partial**; distinguish a successfully searched empty hierarchy or no qualifying evidence in accessible records from failed retrieval. Stop if the root is inaccessible or the remaining evidence cannot support a meaningful account. Always disclose uncertain historical membership. Keep links to external resources as unverified Jira references, never as independent confirmation.
