# Use evidence-bounded historical Jira reporting

CAM-1619 defines progress reports for leadership, where present-day ticket state can misrepresent an earlier reporting period. Reports use Jira evidence recorded by the reporting cutoff to describe period activity and period-end state, rather than combining historical accomplishments with current status or later narrative. This historical contract requires retained change history and explicit uncertainty, but avoids retrospective claims that the available evidence cannot support.

## Boundary and consequences

- The reporting root is an explicit Research Project, Objective, or Epic. Its descendants, including Sub-tasks, define the organizational boundary; ordinary issue links provide context but do not expand it. Related parent and child work supports a single outcome rather than duplicated accomplishments.
- Jira access is read-only, and Jira is the sole evidence source. External links remain unverified references, and reporting does not reconcile PR state, modify tickets, or publish to Jira or external services, even when other workflows would ordinarily perform those actions. Every generated report is saved as a local Markdown file and displayed in chat; read-only Jira access does not prohibit these required local artifacts.
- Current descendant discovery is an accepted limitation when complete historical membership cannot be reconstructed. Reports disclose this limitation rather than claim an exhaustive historical snapshot; unavailable history and text versions remain unknown rather than being replaced with current values.
- Descriptions explain intended work rather than prove completion. Later comments and edits do not silently enter the historical account. Documented contributions remain distinct from assignment-based attribution, comment authorship, and workflow actions. Explicit Jira statements can support attribution to one or more people, but remain recorded claims rather than independently verified facts; citations and material contradictions accompany the claims.
- Assignment-based attribution uses assignment at the accomplishment date for completed work and at the reporting cutoff for outstanding work. Missing historical assignments remain unknown rather than being replaced with today's assignee.
- Lifecycle state and planning expectations are separate dimensions. Explicitly delivered outcomes can be accomplishments within unfinished tickets, while successful completion remains distinct from cancellation. Reopened work appears under its period-end state, with relevant transitions explained rather than counted as repeated accomplishments.
- Partial retrieval produces a clearly qualified partial report when the available evidence still supports a meaningful account. An inaccessible root or insufficient evidence prevents report generation; a failed retrieval never establishes that no work occurred.
